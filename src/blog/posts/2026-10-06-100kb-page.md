Every page on this site has to weigh less than 100 KB before images. That covers the HTML, the stylesheet and whatever JavaScript the page loads.

Right now the heaviest page here comes to about 40 KB, and more than half of that is the stylesheet every page shares. Web fonts are counted separately, since they only load on devices that have nothing suitable installed.

The exact number matters less than having one. Without a budget, every addition looks reasonable on its own. With one, each addition has to pay for itself.

## What counts

Open your browser's developer tools, switch to the Network tab and reload with the cache disabled. The summary at the bottom shows two totals: what was transferred over the network and the size of the resources once unpacked. I budget against the unpacked size, because that is what the browser has to parse, and compression is a bonus on top.

## Where the weight usually comes from

- **Fonts.** Each weight of a web font is a separate download. This site asks for system fonts first and only falls back to a web font where a device has nothing suitable.
- **JavaScript you did not write.** A framework to render text that could have been HTML in the first place.
- **Icons.** An icon font or a large sprite for the six icons a page actually uses. Inline SVG costs a few hundred bytes each.
- **CSS nobody deleted.** Styles for components that left the site two redesigns ago.

## How I keep to it

**System fonts first.** The font stacks here start with the fonts already installed on your device. A web font only loads where those are missing.

**No client-side framework.** Pages are built ahead of time. The shared script on every page does little more than keep a clock running and power the random page button.

**One stylesheet.** Every page uses the same file, so it is downloaded once and served from cache after that.

**Check before shipping.** A budget nobody measures is a wish. I look at the Network tab before publishing anything that adds a file.

## Why bother

Small pages load quickly on slow connections and old phones, and they keep loading quickly as the site grows. They are also easier to work on. There is less to read, less to break and less to explain to the next person, who is usually me, six months later.
